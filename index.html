agentagentstokens
Aaron Levie
ceo @box - your business lives
If you want to know where all the tokens will get consumed in the future, here it is. Agent swarms will have massive implications in a number of enterprise domains like software development, cyber, life sciences research, AI research, finance, and more. The ability to parallelize agents against a unified goal where the volume of work is large, or where the search space not entirely known upfront, is going to be massive. Agents can fan out and coordinate work together on a specific mission in completely different ways from what humans are able to do. Today even in the most resource-abundant organizations, your ability to parallelize work on most projects is quite limited. Humans run into constraints on being able to communicate with one another, share status, deal with conflicts in work, keep shared context, and so on. As AI models can handle larger context without getting confused, and as they’re able to communicate effectively across their work with shared infrastructure, they can avoid many of the pitfalls that people have. You can see how this can lead to not just faster execution of tasks, but qualitatively different outcomes in medicine, research or defending against cyber attacks, scientific and math discoveries, and so on. This is why we’re going to need 1,000X more compute.

agentsmodels
Aaron Levie
ceo @box - your business lives
Good post to start thinking through the long term safety approaches to managing intelligence in an enterprise. As agents begin to do vastly more task execution in the organization than humans, our need to secure, govern, control, and audit that work will become paramount. You’ll have agents operating against most of your most valuable data; they’ll be making decisions that will be harder and harder to reverse engineer; we’ll need to understand the liability and accountability regimes when models make decisions for us; and we need ways of containing the blast radius of accidentally or intentionally malicious agents. “Treating frontier closed and open weight models like insider risks is a way to build such a system. Not because they are necessarily malicious, but because any sufficiently capable actor with access to important systems can make mistakes or be compromised, and the architecture of containment and control must account for that” In a sense, AI will need to have its “zero trust” era that security saw. And all of this leads to needing various layers of protection and auditability of what agents are doing, what data they can work with, and controls for when things go wrong. And a huge opportunity right now for those building such systems across the enterprise.

agentagentsmodel
Madhu Guru
Sr DirectorAI at Meta; Prev: Google - LedVeo
Agent capabilities will soon evolve at RSI pace. Agentic security (identity, permissions, observability) will need to outpace this. The evolutionary direction of the broader agents space is capability -> trust -> autonomy The first phase has been about capability - can the combination of model + harness + tools + context reliably get work done? Capabilities have gotten pretty far, so the focus now shifts to trust…does the harness / system take the right action with right permissions? Can we observe it, audit it and establish accountability? A lot of serious work has already gone into these problems over the last couple of years. Now the challenge gets more important. and harder.
